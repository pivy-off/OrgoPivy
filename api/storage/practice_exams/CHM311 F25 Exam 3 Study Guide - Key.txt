Briefly describe what a polyprotic acid/base is.
Polyprotic acids and bases are compounds that can donate or accept more than one proton.
What does it mean for a molecule to be amphiprotic?
Amphiprotic molecules are those that can behave as an acid and a base by being capable of donating a proton and accepting a proton. The intermediate forms of polyprotic acids and bases are amphiprotic because they are only partially protonated.
The fully protonated form of a polyprotic acid/base behaves as a monoprotic acid.
Briefly state the strategy for dealing with the fully protonated form of a polyprotic acid/base.
For a weak polyprotic acid, or if there is a large degree of separation between Ka1 and Ka2, we know that the first dissociation will usually happen to some extent upon dissolution and the pH will be somewhat acidic, making the second dissociation unlikely. As a result, we can treat the fully protonated form as monoprotic acid, focusing our attention on Ka1 and ignoring Ka2.
The fully deprotonated form of a polyprotic acid/base behaves as a monoprotic base.
Briefly state the strategy for dealing with the fully deprotonated form of a polyprotic acid/base.
For a weak polyprotic base, or if there is a large degree of separation between Kb1 and Kb2, we know that the completely deprotonated form will be incompletely hydrolyzed upon dissolution, and the second form is unlikely to hydroloyze at all. As a result, we can treat the fully deprotonated form as monoprotic base, focusing our attention on Kb1 and ignoring Kb2.
Why can’t we make simple assumptions about the intermediate forms of polyprotic acids/bases and treat them as monoprotic species?
The intermediate forms of polyprotics are amphiprotic, and so behave as both an acid and a base is solution. As a result, we must consider both the dissociation of at least one proton, along with the hydrolysis reaction with water. We can no longer assume the molecule will behave as a monoprotic species, and we resort to using systematic equilibrium.

Explain the difference between electric charge (C), electric current (A), and electric potential (V).
Electric charge is a positive or negative quantity that has the capacity to generate current in a circuit, where current is the ‘flow’ of charge per time. Electric potential refers to the amount of work needed, or the amount of work that can be done, when a unit of charge moves from one point to another.
